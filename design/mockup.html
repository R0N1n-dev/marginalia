Chapter 7

The salt path

By the time the tide withdrew, the path had already changed twice. What had been a grey ribbon at dawn was now a causeway of shining mud, laid out between the marshes like something deliberately offered. Mara stood at the gate with her boots unlaced and counted the gulls, though she could not have said why counting them helped.

The old route ran inland, past the church and the shuttered post office, and everyone in the village still walked it out of habit. But the coastal way was shorter by a mile, and in the weeks since the letter she had stopped trusting habit. She had taken to leaving the house before anyone could ask where she was going — not from secrecy, exactly, but because explaining felt like handing the reason to someone else.

Halfway along the causeway she stopped again. The water had left a line of small shells arranged with a care no one had arranged them in, and she thought of her father's desk, the drawer that only opened when you pressed the underside. Some things keep their shape long after the reason for them has gone.

She wrote the sentence down before she could improve it, then stood up too fast, and the wind took the corner of the page and held it there, trembling, for as long as it pleased.
